## New scholarships added (2)

1. **Lilly Bison Scholarship** (`lilly-bison-scholarship-howard`, `howard-hbcu`). Found via a fresh search combining "Howard University" with recent corporate-gift news rather than the usual scholarship-aggregator phrasing. A $1.1 million gift from Eli Lilly and Company funds up to 12 renewable scholarships/year (up to $10,000 each) across five Howard schools, including the School of Business. Critically, the announcement explicitly lists **rising sophomores** as an eligible class year alongside first-years, rising juniors, and transfers — a precise match for Mason right now. No application portal, deadline, or detailed selection criteria were found in search results; `deadline` set to `null`. Flagged as worth a direct call/email to Howard's Office of Financial Aid or School of Business to find out whether it's folded into the general donor-scholarship application (`howard-business-endowed`, already tracked) or runs as its own process — this is the single most actionable new lead to verify this round.
2. **PIMCO Future Leaders Scholarship** (`pimco-future-leaders`, `finance-major`). A $20,000 scholarship for undergraduate sophomores interested in investment management, administered by Scholarship America. Originally race-targeted (Black/Hispanic-Latinx) at launch in 2021-2022; current published eligibility text no longer states a race restriction, though it still gives preference to federal-financial-aid-eligible and first-generation students. Important nuance surfaced this round: each annual cycle targets a *specific* graduating cohort of sophomores (the 2026 cycle, now closed, targeted students graduating Dec 2027–June 2028 — one year ahead of Mason's ~2029 graduation). So the cycle that just closed (Feb 9, 2026) was never a fit for Mason regardless of timing; the *next* cycle (opening an estimated ~Dec 2026) should target the cohort matching Mason's own graduation timeline. Added with `deadline: null` and a note explaining this cohort-targeting mechanic so a future session doesn't mistakenly treat the next open cycle as "too late" or re-research the eligibility confusion from scratch.
